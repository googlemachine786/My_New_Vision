# Cell Structure

## Introduction

All living organisms are made up of cells. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Cells were first discovered by Robert Hooke in 1665 when he observed thin slices of cork under a microscope. A cell has three main parts: the cell membrane, the cytoplasm, and the nucleus. The cell membrane forms the outer boundary, the cytoplasm is the jelly-like substance that fills the cell, and the nucleus acts as the control centre of the cell.

## The Cell Membrane

The cell membrane, also called the plasma membrane, is the boundary of the cell that controls what enters and leaves the cell. It is a thin, flexible layer that surrounds the cytoplasm and separates the inside of the cell from its surroundings. The cell membrane is selectively permeable, which means it allows only certain substances to pass through it. Useful substances like oxygen, water, and nutrients are allowed in, while waste products are allowed out. This selective control helps the cell maintain a stable internal environment.

## Organelles

Inside the cytoplasm are small structures called organelles, each with a special function. The nucleus is the control centre of the cell; it contains the genetic material (DNA) and directs all the activities of the cell, including growth and reproduction. Mitochondria are known as the powerhouse of the cell because they produce energy in the form of ATP through cellular respiration. Ribosomes are tiny organelles that make proteins for the cell; they can float freely in the cytoplasm or be attached to the endoplasmic reticulum. The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is a network of membranes that transports proteins and other materials within the cell; rough ER has ribosomes attached to it, while smooth ER makes lipids. Chloroplasts are found only in plant cells; they contain the green pigment chlorophyll and carry out photosynthesis to make food. Chloroplasts differ from mitochondria because chloroplasts capture sunlight energy to make glucose during photosynthesis, while mitochondria release energy from glucose during cellular respiration. In this way the two organelles perform almost opposite energy conversions.

## Cell Wall

Plant cells have an additional outer layer called the cell wall, which lies outside the cell membrane. The cell wall of plant cells is made of cellulose, a strong and rigid material. The cell wall gives the plant cell its fixed shape, provides support and protection, and allows the plant to stand upright. Animal cells do not have a cell wall; they only have a cell membrane.

## Cell Types

There are two main types of cells: prokaryotic and eukaryotic. Prokaryotic cells do not have a true nucleus or membrane-bound organelles; their genetic material floats freely in the cytoplasm. Bacteria are examples of prokaryotic cells. Eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus enclosed by a nuclear membrane, along with membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum. Plants, animals, fungi, and protists are made of eukaryotic cells. In short, the key difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells is that eukaryotic cells contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, while prokaryotic cells do not.
