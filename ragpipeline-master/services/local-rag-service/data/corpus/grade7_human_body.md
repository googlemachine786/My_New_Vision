# Human Body

## Digestive System

The function of the digestive system is to break down the food we eat into simpler substances that the body can absorb and use for energy, growth, and repair. Digestion begins in the mouth, where teeth cut and grind food and saliva starts breaking down starch. The food then travels down the food pipe (oesophagus) to the stomach, where digestive juices break it down further. In the small intestine, digestion is completed and nutrients are absorbed into the blood. The large intestine absorbs water, and undigested waste leaves the body.

Enzymes play a very important role in digestion. Enzymes are special proteins that act as biological catalysts, speeding up the breakdown of food into simpler substances without being used up themselves. For example, amylase in saliva breaks down starch into sugars, pepsin in the stomach breaks down proteins, and lipase breaks down fats into fatty acids. Without enzymes, digestion would be far too slow to keep us alive.

## Circulatory System

The circulatory system transports blood, oxygen, and nutrients around the body. The heart pumps blood through blood vessels called arteries, veins, and capillaries. Arteries carry oxygen-rich blood away from the heart to the body, and they have thick, muscular, elastic walls to withstand high pressure. Veins carry oxygen-poor blood back towards the heart, have thinner walls, and contain valves to prevent the backward flow of blood. So the main difference between arteries and veins is the direction of blood flow: arteries carry blood away from the heart while veins return blood to the heart.

Blood contains different types of cells. The function of red blood cells is to carry oxygen from the lungs to all parts of the body. Red blood cells contain a red pigment called haemoglobin, which binds to oxygen and gives blood its red colour. White blood cells fight infections, and platelets help the blood to clot at wounds.
