# Cell Processes

## Transport

Cells constantly move substances in and out through the cell membrane. Diffusion is the movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, until they are evenly spread out. Diffusion does not require energy, so it is a form of passive transport. For example, oxygen diffuses from the lungs into the blood because the concentration of oxygen is higher in the lungs.

Osmosis is the movement of water molecules across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration. Osmosis is a special type of diffusion that involves only water. It is how plant roots absorb water from the soil.

Active transport is the movement of substances from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration, against the concentration gradient. Active transport differs from passive transport because it requires the cell to spend energy (ATP), while passive transport such as diffusion and osmosis happens naturally without using energy. Cells use active transport to take in nutrients even when their concentration outside the cell is low.

## Energy

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food. Photosynthesis occurs in the chloroplasts of plant cells, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll. During photosynthesis, plants use carbon dioxide from the air, water from the soil, and energy from sunlight to produce glucose (food) and oxygen. The word equation is: carbon dioxide + water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, gives glucose + oxygen. The glucose provides energy for the plant, and the oxygen released is used by other living organisms for breathing. Cellular respiration in the mitochondria then breaks down this glucose to release usable energy (ATP) for the cell.
