Executive Summary 
This document provides a complete evaluation reference for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) 
pipelines, covering all six stages: Chunking & Preprocessing, Embedding & Indexing, Retrieval, 
Reranking, Generation Quality, and End-to-End System Metrics. For each stage, the document details: 
• 
Every relevant evaluation metric with mathematical formulas, strengths, and weaknesses 
• 
Alternative metrics and the specific conditions under which each alternative outperforms the default 
choice 
• 
Key research papers that introduced or validated each metric family 
• 
Libraries and tools with the specific capabilities they provide 
• 
Non-functional metrics covering latency, throughput, cost, and robustness 
The critical insight is that no single metric captures the full picture: IR metrics evaluate retrieval only, 
ROUGE and BLEU are surface-level only, BERTScore captures semantic similarity but not factual 
correctness, and LLM judges are subjective and non-deterministic. A robust RAG evaluation stack must 
combine metrics across all stages into a coordinated multi-dimensional assessment.

STAGE 01  ·  CHUNKING & PREPROCESSING 
Splitting raw documents into retrievable units 
Chunking is the first and most consequential decision in a RAG pipeline. Poor chunk boundaries corrupt 
everything downstream — retrieval recall drops because the answer spans two chunks, embedding quality 
degrades because chunks are semantically diluted, and generation suffers from incomplete context. The 
metrics here are largely intrinsic (no ground truth needed) but directly predict downstream performance.

STAGE 02  ·  EMBEDDING & INDEXING 
Encoding chunks into vector space and building the search index 
Embedding quality is the ceiling on retrieval quality — no reranker or prompt engineering can recover 
information lost in a poor embedding. The metrics here split into intrinsic (measuring the embedding space 
geometry) and extrinsic (measuring retrieval performance on benchmark datasets). For Indic language 
pipelines, multilingual benchmarks like MTEB-multilingual and STS-17 are essential additions to the 
standard evaluation suite.

STAGE 03  ·  RETRIEVAL 
Selecting the most relevant chunks from the index given a query 
Retrieval is the make-or-break stage of a RAG pipeline. A chunk not retrieved cannot contribute to the 
answer, making recall the primary objective. But precision matters equally — every irrelevant chunk injected 
into the context window increases hallucination risk and wastes tokens. The metrics in this stage split 
between classical IR metrics (requiring relevance labels) and RAG-specific metrics (some reference-free, 
using LLM judges).

STAGE 04  ·  RERANKING 
Re-ordering retrieved candidates to maximize top-k relevance 
Reranking is a precision amplifier — it does not increase recall (no new documents are added) but 
dramatically improves the ordering of what was retrieved, ensuring the most relevant chunk sits at top-1 
where the LLM will read it first. Cross-encoders achieve this by processing the query and document jointly 
(unlike bi-encoders which encode them independently), but at 5-20x the inference cost. The metrics here all 
measure delta improvement over the first-stage retrieval baseline.

Known LLM Judge Biases (from Zheng et al., 2023) 
Every LLM-as-judge framework inherits these biases from the underlying model. These must be 
mitigated in evaluation design: 
Position Bias: The LLM judge assigns higher scores to options presented first in the prompt. Mitigate by 
randomizing the order of answer presentation across evaluation runs. 
Verbosity Bias: Longer answers score higher regardless of actual quality. Mitigate by normalizing answer 
length or evaluating per-sentence quality rather than whole-answer quality. 
Self-Enhancement Bias: GPT-4 evaluators systematically prefer GPT-4 generated answers over 
equivalently correct answers from other models. Use cross-model evaluation when comparing model 
outputs. 
Sycophancy: LLM judges tend to agree with confident-sounding answers even when they are factually 
wrong. FActScore atomic verification is more resistant to this than holistic G-Eval scoring. 
Domain Knowledge Gaps: LLM judges may be unreliable for highly specialized domains (CBSE-specific 
curriculum, regional languages) where the judge model has limited training data.
